## Principles

- Advance the hidden plots only through causes in the world: preconditions met, actors informed, time passed.
- Reveal signals to the player through reports, envoys and rumours at plausible delays; never skip ahead.
- If the player's actions change a precondition, change the plot; do not force the historical timeline back.
- Keep the player capable of failing. Defeats, disgrace and lost provinces are legitimate outcomes.
- Update hidden variables after each turn; keep them as ranges, not false precision.
- Verification verdicts override earlier DOC/DOCUMENTED labels in legacy notes. A verified date, law or treaty does not verify motives, actual delivery or a counterfactual consequence.
- Never apply a historical future event automatically after divergence. Hypotheses may drive fictional events only with explicit campaign-invention status and a recorded causal basis.
- Do not use authorised troop quotas as mustered strength, whole-army totals as theatre strength, or historical casualties as universal rout thresholds.

## Money and credit

Money exists in four states: promised, granted, collected, paid. Each step takes time and leaks. A promise is not coin.

- Model promised, authorised, collected, remitted and received funds separately. Delays depend on local records and campaign circumstances; there is no verified universal lag.
- Confirmed legal baseline: Hungarian Act VIII protects noble fiscal privileges, while Act LXIII funds infantry and specifies other contributions, including town cash payments. Hungary is not exempt as a whole.
- Credit and arrears can constrain operations; any precise interest, arrears duration or desertion response remains a design hypothesis until supported.
- Occupation may disrupt current collections and seize cash as well as reduce future receipts. Silesia’s precise share of Habsburg revenue remains unverified.
- British subsidy requires separate records of authorisation, remittance and receipt; do not assume automatic delay or automatic timely delivery.

## Bread, forage and the 1740 hunger

Supply scarcity can constrain operations. Specific 1740–41 local harvest failures, grain prices and unrest levels remain unverified in this pass.

- Staff manage bread, forage, transport and quarters. Report consequential shortages and costs to the player.
- Use local campaign evidence for stocks, routes, weather and requisitions. Do not manufacture historical price series or impose a universal few-days supply radius.

## The court and the council

Power runs through the Geheime Konferenz, the ruler's trust, Francis Stephen's position and who controls access. Advice is partisan and often wrong.

- Khevenhüller is listed as Hofkriegsrat Vice-President in the 1740/41 personnel account (KA I pp. 309–310); appointment month and opening-date presence are unverified.
- Exact Conference membership, access and motives require dated evidence. Rivalries and delays are possible campaign developments, not automatic properties of every minister.
- A general’s command and ability to influence the ruler are distinct; track actual delegations and correspondence.

## The great powers

Each power decides from its own interests and what it believes. They do not know what the player knows, and the player does not know what they will decide.

- PC I document 192 dates Gotter’s instructions to 8 December 1740; document 227 allows negotiation for part of Silesia. Do not force his demands to increase after every victory.
- Treaties, perceived interests, resources and information constrain choices; the effectiveness of a player’s deterrence is contingent.
- Sardinian cooperation with Austria began in 1742 according to the Swiss National Museum; the 1743 treaty was not its first involvement.
- Courier delays and interception require a route and circumstances; exact durations remain assumptions.

## Estates, nobles and subjects

Consent has to be bargained for, land by land. Each Estate has its own privileges, grievances and price.

- Different lands and social groups have different legal obligations; do not turn all Estates into one consent switch.
- Hungarian Act LXIII authorises 21,622 infantry in six new regiments and separate mounted obligations. Actual mobilisation and arrival require returns.
- A coronation or homage must be understood within its jurisdiction; a delay does not automatically erase every succession claim.
- Local labour duties, poverty, recruiting and resistance require local evidence; avoid uniform national loyalty or unrest.

## Religion and legitimacy

Religious affiliation, legal rights and institutional interests can matter without determining collective allegiance. Most proposed confessional reactions remain hypotheses.

- Treaty of Berlin (28 July 1742), article VI protects the Catholic religious status quo and Protestant freedom of conscience; enforcement remains a separate question.
- Hungarian Act LXIII sections 13–16 sets obligations for clerical categories. Papal authorisation and total clerical remittances remain unverified.
- The Prague expulsion decree dates to December 1744. Do not project it or allegations of collaboration back into 1741–42.
- Papal neutrality, Hungarian Protestant concessions and Orthodox Frontier rights still need specific dated verification. Treat communities as internally diverse.

## The army and the field

The army on paper is not the army present. Silesia is nearly empty in December 1740; the Prussian army is much larger and ready.

- KA II pp. 58, 66 gives 7,359 in Silesia excluding Glatz, including garrisons, versus at least 26,000–27,000 Prussian invaders in late December 1740. KA I’s 99,446 is the whole Prussian army.
- One Wallis regiment was present in October; 1,719 is its December return, not an October headcount.
- KA II reconstructs Mollwitz strengths at 15,788 Austrian and 21,600 Prussian. These estimates do not define the tabletop demo’s counters.
- The wooden/iron ramrod distinction does not establish sustained rates of fire or damage multipliers. The 1744 resolution is not a date of proven universal delivery.
- Demo movement, fire, morale, casualty and visibility coefficients are uncalibrated design assumptions. Modern map terrain is a proxy. Results inform adjudication; they do not compel campaign outcomes.

## What reaches you, and when

Everything you know is old. Reports have senders, routes and reasons to lie.

- Each report carries a date, a sender and a route; couriers take days; absence of news is not safety.
- Rumours may be true, half true or planted; label confidence honestly.
- The player sees signals only through reports, envoys, letters and scouts.

## Failure remains possible

- A lost or bloody battle: Guns and officers lost, retreat, fortresses exposed, inquiry at court
- Dismissal or loss of command: Recall, reassignment, disgrace; the campaign continues without you or with you in a lesser role
- Court inquiry or imprisonment: Loss of freedom of action; rehabilitation possible but slow
- A province or fortress falls: Revenue, recruiting and prestige drop; allies recalculate
- Money runs out: Mutiny, desertion, forced requisitions, unrest
- Diplomatic isolation: Subsidies and neutrality end; hostile coalition forms
- Your patron falls or turns: Doors close; rivals’ advice prevails
