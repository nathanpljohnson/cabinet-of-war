# Research gaps: Hofkriegsrat Vice-President and Silesian command, Oct-Dec 1740

Method note: Kriegsarchiv Vol. I and Arneth Vol. I were read from archive.org OCR. Duncker (1885) was read from the Czech Ministry of Defence Kramerius OCR. Page numbers are from OCR page markers and could be off by one. Web summaries of Wikipedia were used only as leads.

## GAP 1: Vice-President of the Hofkriegsrat, October 1740

### Finding
**Feldmarschall Ludwig Andreas Graf Khevenhüller. Label: DOCUMENTED (one secondary source that cites primary staff lists, no second independent confirmation found).**

Source: k.k. Kriegs-Archiv (Abtheilung für Kriegsgeschichte), *Oesterreichischer Erbfolge-Krieg 1740-1748*, Vol. I (Vienna 1896), chapter on the Hofkriegsrat, pp. 309-310. Archive.org id `p1sterreichisc01aust` (djvu text).

The text reads: "Im Jahre 1740 (respective 1741) hatte derselbe folgenden Personalstatus: einen Präsidenten seit Ende 1738 (Feldmarschall Joseph Graf Harrach); einen Vice-Präsidenten (Feldmarschall Ludwig Andreas Graf Khevenhüller); 25 (29) Räthe aus dem Herrenstande..."

Its footnote cites "Staats-Kalender oder Hof-Schematismus der Jahre 1740, 1741". The next page (p. 310) says Khevenhüller drew only 800 fl. a year in the post. It notes he was "zugleich commandierender General von Slavonien", and that Feldmarschall Heinrich Daun was "zugleich Stadt-Commandant von Wien".

Calibration:
- The Kriegsarchiv gives "1740 (respective 1741)" with no month. It does not say who was VP on 20 Oct 1740 specifically, or exactly when Khevenhüller was appointed.
- The same volume also confirms Harrach as President "seit Ende 1738". This matches the wiki lists, which give Harrach as President from 1738 (de.wikipedia and Wiener Geschichte Wiki, "Hofkriegsrat"). English Wikipedia's Harrach article says 1739.
- The printed 1740 and 1741 Hof-Schematismus themselves were not seen. They would settle the question.

### Answer to the Khevenhüller question
Yes. The Kriegsarchiv places him as Vice-President in 1740/41 (see above). He had been Field Marshal since 1737 (22 April 1737 per Wurzbach; "1736" in de.wikipedia is probably a slip).

Khevenhüller's own biographies do not mention the post:
- BLKÖ (Wurzbach), "Khevenhüller, Ludwig Andreas", de.wikisource: it says only that he received "die Berufung zum Hofkriegsrathe nach Wien" during the Turkish War, and does not say Vice-President. It also says Charles VI appointed him "Commandant von Wien" before his death. That conflicts with the Kriegsarchiv, which makes Daun the Stadt-Commandant of Vienna. Status: DISPUTED, not resolved here.
- de.wikipedia and en.wikipedia (via fetch summary): no Hofkriegsrat post mentioned. This is silence, not contradiction.

Wikipedia (de and en) gives no Vice-President for 1738-1745, so it is not a usable source for this gap. ÖBL and NDB entries were not found with VP data for 1740.

Anonymous VP memorial (supporting context): in September 1740 a Hofkriegsrat Vice-President wrote a memorandum complaining that referendaries (Wöber) bypassed him. The Kriegsarchiv (Vol. I, pp. 312-314, quoting K.A. Memoiren IX, 56) says the author's name is "nicht bekannt". Do not attribute this to Khevenhüller. Status: UNVERIFIED authorship, although the writer was whoever held the VP post.

## GAP 2: Habsburg command in Silesia and Moravia, Oct-Dec 1740

### General-Militär-Commando in Silesia
**FML Graf Franz Wenzel Wallis, at Gross-Glogau, from June 1740. Label: DOCUMENTED.**

- Duncker, "Die Invasion Schlesiens durch die königlich preussischen Truppen im Monate December 1740", *Mittheilungen des k.k. Kriegs-Archivs* (1885), p. 25: "Das General-Militär-Commando in Schlesien führte seit dem Monate Juni 1740 FML. Graf Franz Wenzel Wallis in Gross-Glogau." The footnote cites the Hofkriegsrat Expedits-Protokoll and Hofkammer-Archiv, 20 Dec 1740.
- The Kriegsarchiv Vol. I, p. 327 agrees. Around 1740 the commanding generals were: Prag, FZM O'Gilvy; Brünn (Spielberg), FML Graf Sinzendorff; Gross-Glogau, FML Graf Wenzel Wallis; Hermannstadt, FM Lobkowitz.
- Arneth also names him ("Franz Wallis") as the Glogau commander; Arneth I, ca. pp. 110-111 and 116 (OCR).
- Name caution: Duncker and the Kriegsarchiv say Wenzel / Franz Wenzel. Do not confuse him with Field Marshal Olivier Wallis of the Turkish war.
- Lentulus: he was a Prussian officer (Robert Scipio von Lentulus in Arneth's Breslau passages), not Austrian. He was not an Austrian commander. Label: DOCUMENTED that no source places him in Habsburg service in Silesia in 1740.

### Glatz
Under the Bohemian General-Kommando, FZM Karl Hermann Graf O'Gilvy (Duncker p. 25). Commandant Oberst von Linckh was ill, so Oberstleutnant von Fontanella was named on 14 Dec (Duncker p. 54). Label: DOCUMENTED.

### Moravia (Olmütz, Brünn)
- Brünn: FML Graf Sinzendorff held the General-Commando around 1740 (Kriegsarchiv Vol. I, p. 327). Label: DOCUMENTED, but "um das Jahr 1740" is vague and no month is given.
- After the loss of Silesia, the remainder of Silesia came under the Brünn command (same page).
- Olmütz: **no commandant found**. UNVERIFIED. Duncker mentions only Batthyány Dragoons and Csáky Hussars being routed "in die Gegend von Olmütz" (p. 61, late Dec).
- Liechtenstein Dragoons: 8 companies entered Silesia and 5 stayed in Mähren (Duncker p. 28).

### Reinforcement timeline (the arrangement changed during the period)
- Conference of 18 Oct 1740 (Vienna): Botta, Browne and Harrach infantry, with Dessewffy Hussars, to march to Silesia. GFWM Preysing supervised the entry (Duncker pp. 27-28). The Kriegsarchiv Vol. I, pp. 368-369 reports a first dislocation order on 15 Oct. DOCUMENTED.
- Regiments arrived in the third decade of November: Botta, then Browne, then Harrach (Duncker p. 28). The Kriegsarchiv says Harrach entered "in der ersten Hälfte des November", and Browne and Botta only in December. This is a minor conflict. Label: DISPUTED between Duncker and the Kriegsarchiv.
- Wallis was designated to stay in Glogau and defend it (Duncker p. 29-30). Browne received the "Interims-Commando" over troops outside Glogau.
- Browne, recalled from Italy (Como), was first meant for Bohemia (14 and 25 Nov). He probably got the Silesia posting at the end of Nov and arrived in the first days of Dec. His earliest surviving report is dated 8 Dec (Duncker p. 33). He received the formal order on 11 Dec (Duncker p. 30, note 1). Label: DOCUMENTED, with the arrival date a SUPPORTED INFERENCE.
- Conflict: Arneth I, ca. p. 117-118, says Browne held the post when the three regiments arrived in the second half of November. Duncker, who worked from the Hofkriegsrat protocols, has the later date. Prefer Duncker.
- 15 Dec: Wallis was instructed by the Hofkriegsrat on what to write to Frederick if Prussia invaded (Duncker p. 52).

### Garrison strength on the eve of invasion
- Duncker p. 55: Wallis Infantry 1,719; Botta, Browne and Harrach 4,770 (August musters, probably lower in fact); Liechtenstein Dragoons (8 companies) about 570 men and 560 horses; free company about 300. Total **7,359 men and 560 horses**. Subtract the Glogau (1,178) and Jablunka (120) garrisons and **about 6,061 men** were under Browne's field command. Duncker notes these figures are largely August/September muster numbers and were probably not reached in December. Label: DOCUMENTED, with an upper-bound caveat.
- Duncker p. 33 (Conference record): about 4,000 imperial soldiers expected to defend Silesia; Browne could muster "nicht viel über 4000 M." for his corps, excluding Glogau (6 Harrach and 4 Wallis companies).
- Arneth I, ca. p. 117: only 3 battalions and 2 grenadier companies in Silesia at the Emperor's death; later (ca. p. 124) Browne retreated behind the Neisse with about 5 battalions (1,600) plus Liechtenstein Dragoons, with the rest in garrisons. By early Feb 1741 he had about 7,500 men (nearly half cavalry).
- Browning (1995, pp. 41-42) via Wikipedia, "Siege of Brieg": 7,500 Austrians under Browne in Silesia. Cited by Wikipedia only (lead); Duncker's 7,359 is consistent. A web summary giving "6,000" is unsourced and not used.
- Duffy, *The Army of Maria Theresa*, and Carlyle were not consulted. The Duffy-derived figures above come only through secondary web summaries and are not used as evidence.

### Browne's dispositions on the eve of the invasion (late Dec)
Troops were concentrated between the Ohlau, the Neisse and the Oder (Duncker pp. 55-56): Liechtenstein Dragoons, with the staff at Würben; Harrach companies at Baumgarten, Ohlau and villages; Wallis and Browne companies at Brieg; Botta at Neisse; Namslau garrisoned by one Wallis company. Duncker says no field artillery appears to have existed. The operations chest was reportedly 10,000 fl. (p. 56). Label: DOCUMENTED.

### What changed after 16 Dec 1740
- The Prussians crossed on 16 Dec (Arneth I, p. 116). Browne left Breslau on 18 Dec and went to Brieg (Duncker p. 55, note 1).
- 22 Dec: the Hofkriegsrat told Browne that FZM Neipperg had been given supreme command over all regiments marching to Silesia. Browne was to keep command "pro interim" until Neipperg arrived (Duncker p. 58). On 23 Dec Maria Theresa named Neipperg commander of the new field army (Duncker p. 58).
- Neipperg waited in Vienna for more than two months before leaving for the army. Arneth I, ca. pp. 123-125 (OCR) says he was made Field Marshal around that time. de.wikipedia says 1741 Generalkommando in Silesia and Mollwitz on 10 Apr 1741. Label: DOCUMENTED for the appointment; SUPPORTED INFERENCE for the arrival date (early 1741), not checked in primary text.
- 28 Dec: the Vienna conference decided to subordinate the Silesian Landesregierung to the commanding general (Duncker p. 57).
- Glogau stormed 9 Mar 1741 (Arneth); the "Wallis went into captivity" detail is in Arneth I, pp. 110-111.
- After the loss of Silesia, the Austrian remnant came under the Brünn General-Kommando (Kriegsarchiv Vol. I, p. 328).
