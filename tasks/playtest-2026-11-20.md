# Cabinet of War — simulated campaign, turns 0–11 (20 Nov 2026)

**Verdict:** the court game works and the history holds. A fresh adjudicator agent on every turn, seeing only the packet, carried the campaign from 21 October to 22 December 1740, through the Prussian crossing of the 16th and Gotter's mission, without one contradiction the checker had to refuse. What broke was the plumbing between the reply format, the old desk and the packet: one blocker (every audience refused), one leak path (demo battlefield sent as live once campaigning began), and a packet that told a fresh chat too little. All of those are fixed on this branch. Two design questions remain for Nathan (illness in 3D, per-place tempo).

## How it was run
- Fresh Playwright profile, sound off, per-turn download off; Nathan's save untouched.
- I played the minister: orders in his own words, orders to people in person and by letter, council courses adopted, a source doubted, a cipher letter given to the clerk, a journey to Brünn and back, own-purse orders.
- Each packet went to a **new** adjudicator agent with no other context — the "fresh chat" case the packet claims to support. Each also wrote feedback on what was unclear.
- Every reply went through the real page: paste → `checkReply` → `mergeTurn` → continuity → accept → walk every day with "Let the day pass".

## History checked
- **Wrong in the opening, now fixed:** Countess Khevenhüller called Maria Theresa "the Empress" (in Oct 1740 the Empress was the late Emperor's widow), and the contract's own example taught the same ("closeted with the Empress"). The contract's purse example named Carinthia, which read as fact about the family's lands.
- **Opening contradicted the repo's own research:** Wallis's letter gave ~3,600 for all Silesia; the Kriegsarchiv figure in `state.json` is 7,359 in late December, Glatz apart. The letter now gives ~7,000 on the rolls and ~3,600 fit. The courier time to Breslau (4–5 days) disagreed with the page (3); aligned.
- **Calendar anchors:** nine marked "not independently checked" are standard dates and now say so. The Lower Austrian homage of 22 Nov 1740 is confirmed ([Wien Museum print](https://sammlung.wienmuseum.at/en/object/1082878-erbhuldigung-der-niederoesterreichischen-staende-fuer-maria-theresia-am-22-november-1740-der-zug-ueber-den-graben/); [NÖ Landesarchiv](https://noe.gv.at/noe/Landesarchiv/Archivalien_vor_den_Vorhang.html)). **Neipperg's command on 22/23 Dec 1740 has no support I could find**; [Wikipedia (Siege of Brieg)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siege_of_Brieg) says Neipperg and Khevenhüller were both considered, and Britannica 1911 has his army collected in February 1741 — now marked disputed (moderate confidence). Added: Frederick leaves Berlin, 13 Dec.
- **Wallis:** the Glogau Wallis is Franz Wenzel, not Georg Olivier of Belgrade; his command of all Silesia rests on one source; [Blumberg](https://warfarehistorynetwork.com/?p=19779) has Browne given the Silesian post in December 1740. Marked disputed.
- **My own doubt was wrong:** Neipperg was held at Brünn after Belgrade ([Austria-Forum](https://austria-forum.org/af/AEIOU/Neipperg%2C_Wilhelm_Reinhard_Reichsgraf/Neipperg%2C_Wilhelm_Reinhard_Reichsgraf_english)); the opening is right.
- **Still unchecked:** the first Conference's seating (21 Oct).

## Still open (ranked)
1. **Illness in 3D (design).** "Ill" only slows his walk. While the story has him in bed for a week he still walks the courtyard and gives orders "in person" in the courtyard. Playtest 1 found the same. Options: wake him in a bedchamber and let people come to him; or keep the courtyard but have the steward turn visitors away. Needs Nathan's call.
2. **Context budget.** The packet reached 97.7k characters (~25k tokens) at turn 10, with almanac entries at 14.9k once the invasion threads went live; replies run 23–45k. One turn costs a chat ~35k tokens, so a 200k chat holds about five turns. The packet is now self-contained enough for a fresh chat each turn — the page should say so to the player.
3. **Scenes are not rare.** The contract says "keep scenes rare"; 8 of 11 replies had one. Either accept that audiences are the game's texture (they were good) or have the checker warn.
4. **One global tempo.** At the crossing Silesia is campaigning while Vienna and the marching regiments are in winter quarters. Per-place tempo would match `world.scene`.
5. **Forwarding notes marked orders answered** (`re` on "your letter went today"). Contract now forbids it; watch whether adjudicators follow.
6. **No almanac baselines** for bread and forage prices, ration sizes, magazine costs, or the actual weather of December 1740 (an open or frozen ditch decides Glogau's siege). Adjudicators invented them.
7. **Untested:** table battle, endings, urgent letters (none was written in 10 turns), forged or intercepted letters, trust ("doubt" was honoured by every later adjudicator).

## Is it fun?
Yes, and more than in October. The court is a machine with friction you can feel: Harrach taking the signature and giving less than asked, the Queen approving routes but not signing, Starhemberg keeping his word "to the florin and no further", the rival Neipperg agreeing to your bargain and then telling the Grand Duke it was his own plan. The best moment came from the dice: the express that would have moved the Queen arrived two days late because the courier fell on the ice. The weak point is the same as before but smaller: the 3D world shows the place and the season, but not the minister's condition.
